# Plan: the hold drops to 0.6 s, and the tests that only did arithmetic pin 0.8

Refs #138. The issue stays open until the before/after protocol (step 8)
has judged a block of 50 `heard` lines at 0.6 s. Base: `main` `be27af4`
(v2.0.0, #80 merged in PR #134), which is the base of
`perf/138-end-of-turn`. 1084 tests pass there (782 subtests). Every
`file:line` below was checked against `be27af4`. This plan lands **first**,
before #135, #136 and #137.

Files that change: `src/omarchy_voice/config.py`,
`share/config.example.toml`, `tools/timing_report.py` (docstring only),
`tests/test_config.py` and `tests/test_local_engine.py`. Nothing changes in
`pyproject.toml`, `nix/`, `listen_local.py`, `local_engine.py` or
`README.md`.

## Approved decisions, carried over from the spec

1. **The 0.6 s default ships first and alone, and the default moves, not
   only this machine's `config.toml`.**
   - Alone: it is the only candidate with a cost known in advance (none: no
     dependency, no new code path), undone without a release. A detector in
     the same change would make any change in the fragment rate impossible
     to attribute.
   - The default, because a line in `~/.config/omarchy-voice/config.toml`
     is unreviewed, unversioned and reaches one host. The default reaches
     p620 and razer on the next rebuild, and `git revert` undoes it. #72 set
     0.8 s the same way.
   - The owner overrode #80's "wait for a 0.8 s block" trigger. The
     reference is the existing baseline, 3.2 % (3 of 94) to 3.9 % (4 of
     102), counted by the same rule under longer holds.
   - Thresholds are #80's: **2 or fewer** fragment endings in a block of 50
     `heard` lines is acceptable, **4 or more** is a cut-off, 3 is
     inconclusive and the next 50 decide.
   - **Rollback when cut-offs rise:** 4 or more in the first block of 50 at
     `hold=0.60s` means the user sets `end_of_speech_seconds = 0.8` under
     `[ears]` in `config.toml` that day (no rebuild; restart
     `omarchy-voice`, the config is read at start), and a one-line revert of
     the default follows as its own PR. That block is also decision 3's
     trigger.
2. **"Hold on after a trailing fragment" is not built now.** Its trigger is
   decision 3's. Its shape is fixed for the follow-up: not "transcribe, then
   reopen the capture" (closing `pw-record`, `listen_local.py:334-339`, the spec's `:333-338` is one line early,
   loses the words said in the hole and costs a second whisper pass).
   Instead, `record_utterance` asks a caller-supplied
   `should_stop(pcm) -> bool` at the hold without closing the microphone;
   the engine's version transcribes and returns `not is_fragment(text)`; a
   fragment keeps capturing up to one extra hold; `is_fragment` moves from
   `tools/timing_report.py` into the package. Cost: one whisper pass per
   fragment, a `record_utterance` signature change that
   `tools/bench_local.py --live` also sees, and it only catches the listed
   words. Not now: at 3-4 % fragment endings, 96 turns in 100 would pay for
   nothing.
3. **smart-turn v3 is deferred. Exact trigger:** 4 or more fragment endings
   in a block of 50 `heard` lines at any `end_of_speech_seconds` of 0.8 s
   or less, counted by `python3 tools/timing_report.py --since <day>` with
   `trace_timings = true`. When it fires, a new intent weighs smart-turn
   against decision 2's shape; decision 2 is the default choice unless the
   block's fragments are mostly clean-looking half sentences. Not now:
   `onnxruntime`, `numpy`, a numpy mel extractor and 8 MB of weights not in
   nixpkgs, for a failure never seen at 0.8 s. Recorded for the follow-up:
   weights pinned in `nix/package.nix` by `fetchurl` and hash, never
   downloaded at run time; opt-in `end_of_turn = "smart-turn"` (default
   `"hold"`); the mel extractor tested against a committed `transformers`
   fixture; it runs through the same `should_stop` seam as decision 2.
4. **The wake path follows the same hold. No separate knob.** `_wake_turn`
   reads `self.config.end_of_speech_seconds` (`local_engine.py:477`), so
   the default reaches it with no code. Its failure is benign: "Oma" and a
   pause only switches listening on (`:490`), and the next turn hears the
   instruction. A new test pins the wake path to the configured hold.
5. **#128 stays separate.** No line of `_wake_turn` changes: `opened`,
   `capped` (`:478-482`) and the echo tail wait are untouched. A shorter
   hold moves a one-breath wake capture further from `wake_max_seconds`, not
   closer.
6. **"Cut off" is measured by #80's `is_fragment` count
   (`tools/timing_report.py:51`), and nothing new.** It is a lower bound,
   but the baseline was counted the same way. No "repeated within seconds"
   signal. **`trace_timings` is turned on on this machine now**, by the
   owner, as a manual step outside the diff. Whatever accumulates at 0.8 s
   before the merge is the before block, even if shorter than 50.
7. **CPU budget, for decision 2 or 3's follow-up only:** the added time per
   pause at p95, on each of p620 and razer, is under a quarter of the hold
   it saves (under 0.05 s against the 0.2 s from 0.8 to 0.6), measured by a
   bench over synthetic audio (her TTS voice and silence), never a live
   capture.
8. **Approved with (2026-09-24): only the 0.6 s default ships here.**
   "Holding on after a trailing fragment" (decision 2) and smart-turn v3
   (decision 3) wait for the written trigger. No step below builds any part
   of them, not even the `should_stop` seam or the move of `is_fragment`.
