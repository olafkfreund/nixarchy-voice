# Intent: Does anything replace tesseract as the model-independent floor?

> Rewritten. The first version of this file asked which vision model should
> replace tesseract. That was the wrong question, and the measurements taken to
> answer it are what show why. The "return the image" half has moved to #58,
> which is the stronger of the two.

## The question this issue is actually asking

Not "which vision model is best". We do not choose the model.

Both repos expose MCP servers, so the model belongs to whoever is calling —
Claude for one user, opencode with a local model for another. Anything we build
on top of a *particular* model's abilities is a bet on a choice that is not ours
and that changes per user.

The real question is narrower and still worth answering: **is there a
model-independent screen reader better than tesseract?** Something every caller
gets identically, offline, with no account and no API key.

## Why the answer is probably no, and the evidence is the spread

Measured on one 2560x1440 capture, single-word localisation against tesseract's
own word boxes as ground truth:

| model | where | single-word boxes in-box | median offset |
|---|---|---|---|
| `qwen3.5:cloud` | cloud | 17/18 | **0.1 wh** |
| `minimax-m3:cloud` | cloud | 16/24 | 1.5 wh |
| `gemma4:cloud` | cloud | 5/24 | 4.0 wh |
| `qwen3.8:27b` | local | 10/12 | 0.2 wh (at 17.0 s **per word**) |
| `gemma4:12b` | local | — | 4–12 wh |
| `gemma4:e4b` | local | — | 16–110 wh |

**0.1 to 110 word-heights.** That three-order-of-magnitude spread is the finding,
and it is an argument about architecture rather than a leaderboard. `click_text`
clicks the centre of a box with no verification (`tools.py:2408`); an error of
one word-height lands on the neighbouring row and the agent is told it clicked
the thing it asked for. A component that silently does that on some users'
machines and not others is worse than a component that is uniformly mediocre.

Tesseract's boxes, whatever the quality of its characters, are the same for
everyone.

## What is genuinely wrong with tesseract, and where it actually hurts

The text. On our own UI: `wordow`, `Toaay`, `1ssues-«`, `Xpiinsitbelpackaging`
(#32). And OCR is 34–38% of task time (#39).

But the damage is not where it looks. `_find_phrase` matches the caller's phrase
against tesseract's tokens, so when tesseract reads `wordow`, `click_text("window")`
does not misclick — it finds nothing and reports "could not find it on screen".
**Bad characters cost `click_text` recall, not accuracy.** That is a different
defect from the one the issue originally described, and it has a much cheaper fix
than replacing the engine.

## What was measured and should not be re-measured

- `glm-ocr` (1.6 GB, purpose-built) transcribes **visibly better than tesseract** —
  clean URLs, full sentences, none of the noise above — but cannot take a
  full-resolution frame at all: 2560x1440 aborts with `token repeat limit reached`
  in 1.5 s, and `repeat_penalty` does not change it, so it is resolution-driven.
  A 1280x720 crop works (6362 chars, 6.7 s), implying ~27 s of tiling per screen
  against tesseract's 4.19 s. Better reader, worse floor.
- Cloud works: `api-ollama` in agenix authenticates against
  `https://ollama.com/api/chat`. It is not a candidate for the floor, because a
  floor cannot require an account, a network, or uploading the user's screen.
- One call for a whole screen beats per-word decisively. The original probe's
  per-word framing made vision look worse than it is.

### Three ways the comparison failed silently, all toward "vision is bad"

Recorded because anyone re-running this will hit them, and each produced a wrong
number that looked plausible:

1. **Key names vary.** `qwen3.5` emits `text_`/`box_` with trailing underscores.
   A parser expecting `text`/`box` scored the most accurate model in the set at
   **0/0**.
2. **Granularity varies.** Models return line-level boxes as readily as word-level
   (mean 2.2–2.9 words/box, max 15). Scoring a line's centre against one word's
   box turned 17/18 at 0.1 wh into 21/41 at 2.2 wh.
3. **A model can narrate instead of answering.** `glm-5.3-flash` had the best text
   recall of anything tested (96.0%) and scored zero by replying with 26 kB of
   prose starting "Let me extract all text elements from this image".

## Proposed outcome

One of two, and I expect the first:

1. **Tesseract stays, and this issue closes with the reasoning recorded** — that
   the floor must be model-independent, that the spread above is why, and that
   the text-quality complaint is better addressed by #58 (let capable callers see
   the pixels) plus a `click_text` fallback for the recall failure.
2. Or a model-independent local reader is found that beats tesseract on text
   without losing box quality or the offline/no-account property. `glm-ocr` was
   the best candidate and failed on frame size.

## Affected users and systems

`_ocr_words` and `_read_screen_text` in `tools.py`. No user-visible change unless
outcome 2.

## Constraints

- The floor must work offline, with no account, no API key, and no model choice.
- `click_text` boxes must not vary with the caller's model.
- Measured on tasks, not a stopwatch on one image (#23, #32).
- More than one screen (#32: overlap swings 67–84% with content).

## Open questions

1. **Is outcome 1 acceptable without a full campaign?** The architectural argument
   does not need more measurement — it needs the spread, which we have. A campaign
   would refine numbers that cannot change the conclusion.
2. **Does the `click_text` recall fallback belong here or in its own issue?**
   It is the concrete fix for the real defect and depends on neither outcome.
3. **Is `glm-ocr` worth a second look tiled**, purely as a `read_screen` option
   for offline users, accepting ~27 s?
