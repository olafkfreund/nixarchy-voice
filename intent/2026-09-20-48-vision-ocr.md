# Intent: Measure a vision model against tesseract, on our own tasks

## Problem

OCR is the dominant cost in a desktop task and the least trustworthy part of it.
The #39 trace, over real tasks:

| task | total | ocr | subprocess |
|---|---|---|---|
| read screen → heading | 12.66 s | **4.19 s** | 0.08 s |
| read screen → titles | 14.33 s | **5.40 s** | 0.09 s |
| read screen → focus | 12.18 s | **4.93 s** | 0.09 s |

34–38% of a task, against `capture` at 0.06 s and `hyprctl` at 0.03 s. And the
tokens it returns on our own UI include `wordow`, `Toaay`, `1ssues-«`,
`Xpiinsitbelpackaging` (#32).

We have never measured the alternative. #23's standard is that a change this
size must be argued from task outcomes, and we have numbers on neither side.

## What a read-only probe already established

Nothing was changed and nothing was committed but this file. Recorded here
because it reshapes the question rather than answering it.

**Three vision-capable models are already on the host** — `gemma4:e4b`,
`gemma4:12b`, `qwen3.8:27b` (ollama, `capabilities` includes `vision`). So the
per-frame cost the issue worried about is zero on the local path, and no pixels
leave the machine. That is a different cost shape from the paid API the issue
assumed, and it removes cost as the deciding axis for the local half.

**Localization is the crux, and it separates the models.** `click_text` does not
want prose; `_find_phrase` takes tesseract word boxes and clicks the centre of
the matched span (`tools.py:2408`), blind, with no verification that the click
landed on the right thing. Asked for the box of a known word on one 2560×1440
capture, against tesseract's own boxes as position ground truth:

| model | offset from true centre | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `gemma4:e4b` | 16–110 word-heights | localization is noise |
| `gemma4:12b` | 4–12 word-heights | reads text, cannot place it |
| `qwen3.8:27b` | 0.0–1.8 word-heights | genuinely localizes |

**For the one model that can, the operating point is a straight trade, and it
turns on capture resolution** — same 12 words, same prompt, same model:

| capture | in-box | median offset | per word |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1280 wide (hermes-companion's number) | 5/12 | 1.1 word-heights | **3.2 s** |
| full 2560 wide | **10/12** | **0.2** word-heights | **17.0 s** (9.7–40.8) |

The downscale was my own confound, not a property of the model: 1280 halves a
13 px row to 6.5 px, and every clean hit at 1280 was large text. hermes-companion
picked 1280 to *describe* a frame, which is a different job from clicking one.

So on this evidence neither vision operating point beats tesseract for
`click_text`: the fast one is too imprecise to click (1.1 word-heights on a 13 px
row lands on the next row), and the precise one costs 17.0 s **per word** on
average against tesseract's 4.19 s for **all 119**.

Two words — `account` and `Eaters` — missed at *both* resolutions, and they were
the only two that did. A consistent failure is a cause, not variance, and it is
exactly what a mean hides. The likeliest explanation is that the word also occurs
elsewhere on the screen below tesseract's confidence floor, so the model
localized a real second instance and the "ground truth" is what is wrong. If so
that is a methodology bug, not a model limit, and the campaign has to handle
duplicate text explicitly rather than scoring it as a miss.

That is one screen. #32 is the standing example of a wrong conclusion drawn from
exactly that much evidence, so it is stated here as a hypothesis to test, not a
result.

## Proposed outcome

A recorded comparison on our tasks that either clears #23's bar or records why
tesseract stays — with the honest expectation, from the above, that
`read_screen` and `click_text` get **different answers**. A model that reads a
screen well and localizes slowly is an upgrade to one and a regression to the
other.

## Affected users and systems

`_ocr_words` and `_read_screen_text` in `tools.py`; every tool that reads or
clicks. No user-visible change unless the numbers clear the bar.

## Constraints

- Measured on tasks, not a stopwatch on one image (#23, and #32's example).
- More than one screen, because #32 showed content swings the numbers.
- `click_text` needs boxes. Prose does not substitute.
- Must not regress the privacy guards — a vision path is another capture seam.

## Open questions

1. **Scope to the local models, or also the claude-code backend vision path?**
   Local is free and private; the backend draws on the plan and is the only one
   that would show what a frontier model does. I lean local-only for the
   comparison, and one backend sample for reference.
2. **Is a one-shot "all words with boxes" variant in scope?** Per-word at 17 s mean
   is hopeless, but one call returning every box would change the cost model
   entirely. Untested. I think it must be in scope or the comparison is rigged.
3. **How many screens is enough** to not repeat #32's mistake? I propose three
   of different density, each with a stated word count.
4. **Does `read_screen` get evaluated separately**, given it needs no boxes at
   all and may be the half that wins?
