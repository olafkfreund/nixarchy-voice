# Intent: end the turn sooner without cutting the user off

Refs #138. Follow-up from #80 (PR #134), which measures the real ENDPOINT
wait and counts fragment endings but ships no detector. Line numbers are
from `main` at `be27af4` (v2.0.0).

**Decision recorded here: the owner chose on 2026-09-24 to build this now.**
That overrides #80's trigger. #80's approved spec and plan
(`spec/` and `plan/2026-09-24-80-learned-end-of-turn.md`, decision 5) said
the follow-up starts only after **4 or more fragment endings in a block of
50 `heard` lines**, with `trace_timings` on, at a hold of 0.8 s or less.
That has not happened. The trigger is not met and not measured. This intent
goes ahead anyway, by the owner's choice, and says so rather than claiming
the evidence asked for it.

## Problem

The local engine still ends a sentence after one fixed stretch of quiet.
#80 explained why one number cannot fit both a thinking pause and a
finished sentence. It then built the measurement, not the fix.

How the hold works today:

- `end_of_speech_seconds: float = 0.8` (`src/omarchy_voice/config.py:361`).
  "Loud" is one RMS threshold, `silence_level = 0.02` (`config.py:357`).
- `listen_local.record_utterance` (`listen_local.py:287`) reads 50 ms frames
  from `pw-record` (16 kHz mono, `:34-35`, `:299`). A frame above the
  threshold moves `heard_at`. After that, the capture stops once
  `now - heard_at > hang` (`:323-331`), then `pw-record` is terminated with
  a wait of up to 2 s (`:333-338`). Pausing, trailing off and finishing all
  look the same.
- The engine passes the configured hold for normal turns
  (`local_engine.py:422`, `:432`) and for wake-word turns (`:477-479`).
- #80 added `self._last_loud` (`local_engine.py:391`, `:397`), set on every
  frame above `silence_level`. `_hear` starts the ENDPOINT span there
  (`:528`) and the TIMING line prints `hold=`.
- The engine only knows the words after whisper has run, and whisper runs
  after the cut. Anything based on words comes too late to keep the
  microphone open.

What "fragment ending" means (`tools/timing_report.py:45-56`,
`is_fragment`): the heard text ends with `,`, `...`, `…`, `-` or `—`, or its
last word is one of `and or but the a an to of so then with because if`.
It is a heuristic and a lower bound. "Open the browser", said when the user
meant "…on workspace two", is not caught. The tool prints counts only,
never what was heard.

What the data says today (checked 24 Sep 2026, read-only, no microphone):

- #80's spec baseline: **3 fragment endings in 94 `heard` lines**, all
  under older, longer holds.
- `tools/timing_report.py` over the whole `session.log` now reports
  **4 of 102 (3.9 %)**. **0 `heard` lines** are stamped after #72 merged
  (24 Sep 12:26), so 0.8 s has still not been heard on a real turn.
- **0 `TIMING` lines and 0 `hold=` values.** `trace_timings` is off
  (`config.py:419`, default `False`, not set in this machine's
  `config.toml`). No real endpoint time exists yet.

So the cost of the hold (dead air on every turn) is known by construction,
but how often it cuts the user off, and how much of the wait is the hold
rather than teardown, is still unmeasured.

## Proposed outcome

- A turn that has clearly finished reaches whisper sooner than it does with
  today's 0.8 s hold.
- A pause in the middle of an instruction is cut off **no more often** than
  with the fixed hold. Measured with #80's tool: 2 or fewer fragment endings
  per 50 `heard` lines stays "acceptable".
- The effect is readable from real turns: `TIMING` lines with `hold=` and
  the endpoint, grouped by the report, before and after.
- If any detector is missing, fails or is slow, the engine falls back to the
  fixed hold. Listening never stops because of it.

Candidates, in the order #138 names them. None is chosen here:

1. **`end_of_speech_seconds = 0.6`.** A default change, no new code path.
   Saves 0.2 s per turn if it does not cut the user off. #80's spec rejected
   doing it "now" as a guess; its procedure reached 0.6 only after 50
   acceptable lines at 0.8.
2. **smart-turn v3** (pipecat-ai). Checked 24 Sep 2026:
   - BSD-2-Clause. About 8M parameters, Whisper-Tiny encoder with a linear
     classifier. **8 MB int8 ONNX**, 32 MB unquantized. Current release
     line is v3.2; the reference `inference.py` loads `smart-turn-v3.1.onnx`.
   - Input: 16 kHz mono PCM, up to 8 s, zero-padded at the front. That is
     the format `record_utterance` already captures.
   - Runtime: `numpy`, `onnxruntime`, and `transformers`'
     `WhisperFeatureExtractor` for the mel features. `transformers` is a
     very large dependency for one feature extractor; the mel step would
     likely need reimplementing in numpy.
   - CPU cost, as published: "as little as 10ms on some CPUs, and under
     100ms on most cloud instances" (a blog title says 12 ms). Not measured
     on this machine.
   - It is meant to run once per pause, after a VAD sees silence, not on
     every frame. Here that would be after a short quiet in
     `record_utterance`, deciding whether to stop or keep holding.
   - Nix: `onnxruntime` and `python3xxPackages.onnxruntime` 1.27.1 are in
     nixpkgs unstable. **smart-turn itself is not in nixpkgs**; the weights
     would be fetched and pinned in `nix/package.nix`.
3. **Hold on after a trailing fragment.** The lighter alternative #138
   names: when the transcript ends in a fragment-like word
   (`is_fragment`), keep listening instead of ending the turn. No model, no
   new dependency. But the words exist only after the cut, so this really
   means "transcribe, see a fragment, reopen the capture, stitch the two
   clips" (#80 spec decision 3). It adds a second whisper pass and a gap
   while the microphone was closed, and it only helps pauses whose first
   half ends on a listed word.

Silero VAD (through whisper-cpp `--vad`) and the LiveKit turn-detector were
rejected in #80's spec and stay rejected: the first works on a finished
clip, not a live capture; the second needs streaming text, is not OSI
licensed, and is 137-165 MB.

## Affected users and systems

- `listen_local.record_utterance` (the stop rule), `local_engine.py`
  (`_record`, `_turn`, `_wake_turn`, `_hear`), `config.py`
  (`end_of_speech_seconds`, any new knob), `share/config.example.toml:91`.
- `tools/timing_report.py`, which is how any change is judged.
- `pyproject.toml` (depends only on `mcp>=1.2`) and `nix/package.nix`, if a
  model or runtime is added.
- The wake-word path, which uses the same hold and whose `capped` check
  (`local_engine.py:478-491`) decides whether a one-breath instruction is
  acted on.
- The user of this machine, who waits through the hold on every turn and is
  the one cut off when it is wrong.

## Constraints

- **Never record the user to evaluate this.** No test or bench captures the
  microphone. Evidence comes from the user's own normal `heard` and `TIMING`
  lines, counted by the report tool, which prints counts and not content.
  Tests use fakes and the stepped clock.
- **No new heavy dependency without an explicit decision** (#80 intent
  constraint). An ONNX runtime is a real change to what the package is.
  `transformers` or PyTorch at run time is not acceptable for a feature
  extractor.
- Offline and local. Any model is pinned by Nix, never downloaded at run
  time. No audio leaves the machine.
- CPU cost: a detector must spend less time per pause than the hold it
  saves, on this machine (#138, #80 spec decision 5).
- Fallback to the fixed hold on any detector failure.
- Build on `main` as it is after #80 (`_last_loud`, `hold=`). #114 rewrote
  `_record`; do not undo its echo tail wait or `_ShutForHer`.

## Open questions

1. **Ship the 0.6 s default alone first?** It is a one-line default change
   (plus the example config and a test) with no dependency. It could land
   first and be judged with #80's report over the next 50 lines, with a
   detector only if 0.6 cuts off. Or does the owner want a detector in the
   same change? And should the default move, or only this machine's
   `config.toml`?
2. **Is a detector a new dependency worth its cost?** smart-turn means
   8 MB of weights in the Nix closure, `onnxruntime` as a runtime
   dependency, and a mel feature extractor written or vendored to avoid
   `transformers`. Against that, "hold on after a trailing fragment" needs
   nothing new but costs a second whisper pass and only catches listed
   words. Which, if either, is worth building?
3. **How is "cut off" measured without recording the user?** Today it is
   `is_fragment` over `heard` lines: a lower bound that misses clean-looking
   half sentences. Is that enough, or is another signal wanted that also
   records no audio (for example, the user repeating or finishing the
   instruction in the next turn within a few seconds, counted not printed)?
   Should `trace_timings` be turned on on this machine now, so the 0.8 s
   baseline exists before anything changes?
4. **The wake path.** It shares the hold through `_record`. Does it opt in
   to the shorter hold or detector, or keep the fixed hold? A shorter hold
   there makes a one-breath wake instruction more likely to end early,
   and `capped` then decides whether it is acted on.
5. **#128 as related work.** `_wake_turn` starts `opened` before `_record`,
   so since #114 `capped` also counts the echo tail wait, and a complete
   instruction right after a reply can be dropped to "wake only". Any change
   to how the wake capture ends touches the same lines. Fix #128 first, fold
   it in, or keep it separate?
6. **What CPU budget is acceptable?** If smart-turn is chosen, how is its
   CPU time per pause measured on this machine (a bench over synthetic or
   existing non-user audio, never a live capture), and what is the bar:
   less than the 0.2-0.4 s it would save, or a stricter one?
